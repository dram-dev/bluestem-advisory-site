Rooted

Whatever form your strategic plan takes, it's alive: people can say why, the work has owners and dates, and leadership and the board see the same picture each quarter. That's rarer than you'd think. What we'd look at with you is the ground under it — which of the things it rests on are still assumptions rather than facts, and who you haven't heard from lately. That's what carries a plan through a change of leadership, and it's usually a short piece of work.

Taking hold

There's a real direction here, and parts of it are working. Where things in this band tend to go quiet is between the intention and the calendar: work that belongs to a team rather than a person, measures without a starting number, a plan the board approved and leadership hasn't looked at since. None of that is hard to fix — and if your plan lives mostly in conversation, writing those few things down is most of the job. We'd start with owners and first moves, ninety days by date, and build the quarterly look-back around them.

Still underground

The direction is in the soil more than above it — and that's true whether it's a strategic plan nobody opens or a shared understanding nobody has written down. Most of the organizations we're proudest of working with started here. It isn't a strategy problem so much as a ground problem: the why hasn't been said out loud together, so nothing beneath it connects. We'd start at the root — a short conversation about what the organization is truly for — before writing anything. It takes less time than people fear, and it usually goes better than people expect.

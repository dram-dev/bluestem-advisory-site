To start

First — where does your strategic plan live today?

Question 1 of 10

If you stopped three people in the hallway — a board member, a manager, someone on the front line — and asked why the organization is heading where it's heading, what would you hear?

Question 2 of 10

Think of one thing in your strategic plan — or one thing the organization has decided to do this year — a project, a change, a push. Who owns it?

Question 3 of 10

What has to happen in the next ninety days for that to move — and by when?

Question 4 of 10

How will you know the plan is working — and do you know where you started?

Question 5 of 10

When did leadership and the board last look together at where the organization is heading — the direction, not the budget?

Question 6 of 10

Some of what your strategic plan rests on you know, and some you believe. Could you point to which is which?

Question 7 of 10

Who was asked what they needed before the direction was set — and who was on the list and never heard from?

Question 8 of 10

What did you decide not to do — and could leadership explain why to the board, or to anyone who asked?

Question 9 of 10

When did one of your leaders last have a real conversation with a member or customer they didn't already know personally?

Question 10 of 10

If the one person who holds a key process together left this month, what happens?

Where should the written version go?

You'll see your result on the next page. Leave an email and the written version — a line on where your plan lives, your band, and the three answers that pulled the score down — arrives from us within a minute.

A note on the score: it asks whether your direction is alive — owned, dated, measured, looked at, and understood by more than the people who set it — not whether it's clever, and not whether it's written down. Ten questions can't see your organization; they can only ask what you'd say out loud.
